My primary research focus is audio and signal processing for medical AI, mainly the analysis of cough and lung sounds recorded with smartphones and wearables. Most of this work deals with data that are scarce, noisy, and recorded under varying conditions, so I work on generative data augmentation, domain adaptation across recording devices and auscultation sites, and models small enough to run on the device.

Within medical AI, I am particularly interested in:

- respiratory sound analysis for diseases such as asthma, COPD, and pneumonia;
- multimodal models that combine lung sounds, chest X-rays, and clinical biomarkers;
- ocular imaging for non-invasive jaundice assessment;
- physiological and motion signals from wearables, and their relation to environmental exposure.

A goal that runs through this work is respiratory sound classifiers whose performance holds across microphones, recording sites, and patient populations, not only on the dataset they were trained on.

Outside medical AI, my master's thesis was on graph-based information retrieval, published in [Expert Systems with Applications](/publications/#graph-based%20extension).
